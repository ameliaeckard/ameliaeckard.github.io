Emergency Fund Predictor

Machine Learning on Federal Reserve Survey Data

Python scikit-learn pandas

The Question

Can a household cover a sudden $400 expense using cash or savings alone, without borrowing or selling something? This is the Federal Reserve's standard measure of financial fragility in the annual Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (SHED), and it is a sharper test of financial resilience than income alone.

The Approach

Several classification models were trained on 12,295 SHED survey respondents, using demographic, income, and financial behavior features to predict whether a respondent could cover the expense from cash or savings. Models were compared on accuracy, precision, and recall, with attention to which features carried the most predictive weight.

Key Finding

56.3%

of respondents could not cover a $400 emergency expense with cash or savings alone.

12,295

respondents in the training and evaluation dataset, drawn from the Federal Reserve SHED survey.

Why It Matters

Financial fragility at this scale is a policy-relevant problem, not just a modeling exercise. Understanding which factors predict the ability to absorb a small financial shock helps identify where interventions, emergency savings programs, access to short-term credit, financial literacy resources, would have the most impact.
