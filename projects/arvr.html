Spatial Audio AR/VR Research

Accessible Indoor Navigation on Apple Vision Pro

Swift ARKit RealityKit Computer Vision

The Problem

Indoor navigation is a persistent accessibility gap for people who are blind or have low vision. GPS does not work reliably indoors, and most existing tools rely on pre-mapped environments or infrastructure that most buildings do not have. The goal of this research is a system that works in unfamiliar indoor spaces using only the sensors already built into a headset.

The approach combines real-time object detection with directional 3D audio, so that a user hears where obstacles and points of interest are relative to their own position, rather than reading a screen or feeling for a cane tap.

How It Works

ARKit's scene understanding and object detection identify obstacles, doorways, and landmarks in the environment in real time. Each detected object is assigned a position in 3D space, and RealityKit's spatial audio engine renders a directional sound cue anchored to that position, so a user hears a doorway "to the left and ahead" the same way they would hear a person speaking from that direction.

The system is being built and evaluated on Apple Vision Pro, chosen for its sensor suite and headroom for on-device processing without needing a connection to an external server.

Targets

Identification Accuracy

Targeting 85% accuracy identifying obstacles and points of interest in unfamiliar indoor spaces.

Navigation Speed

Targeting 30% faster navigation through unfamiliar indoor spaces compared to unassisted wayfinding.

Status

This is active undergraduate research at UNC Charlotte, developing Swift-based prototypes and iterating on detection accuracy and audio cue design. Source and progress notes are tracked on GitHub.
